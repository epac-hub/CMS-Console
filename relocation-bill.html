The Healthcare Paradox

The Relocation Bill

One ticket, one flight, no passport needed — and a 1.68× surcharge included.
A finding of the CMS Console · healthconsole.org · every figure verified against the primary federal file
Medicare’s underpayment of Puerto Rico does not save the federal government money — it relocates the cost: every senior who leaves the island becomes, on arrival in a state, the same beneficiary in the same program at a benchmark averaging 1.68 times what CMS pays in Puerto Rico. As of 2024 — the most recent year the Census Bureau has published — 473,944 Puerto Rico–born people aged 65+ reside across the 50 states and DC, up 41% (+137,489 people) since 2013; the surveys recorded ~84,400 seniors 65+ moving directly from the island over those eleven years, the rest of the increase being island-born residents already stateside crossing age 65. At their states’ CY2027 benchmarks that population represents about $3.02 billion a year more than the identical people would represent at Puerto Rico’s benchmark. Paying fairly in Puerto Rico costs less than paying 1.68 times as much for every senior the underpayment pushes to a state.

What the numbers mean

  • Puerto Rico’s CY2027 benchmark averages $777.98/month — the lowest in the nation. The states these seniors moved to average $1,309.09: 1.68× the price for the identical beneficiary.
  • These are U.S. citizens relocating within their own country. No visa, no eligibility change — only the benchmark changes, the moment the address does.
  • The next decade of the bill is already in place: 287,302 Puerto Rico–born residents aged 55–64 already live across the states (2024 files).
  • Read it honestly: benchmark-differential arithmetic, not a payment forecast — actual payments run through bids, rebates, bonuses and risk adjustment. Full method and caveats on the console.
